# Research protocol

Use the corrected optical and planetary inputs. Keep the two published Juice witnesses and their 2 arcsecond cap. Measure how position and velocity balls at first reception expand through the flyby. Start with radii 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1 and 10 km with velocity radius equal to the position radius divided by 100,000 seconds. Preserve unresolved cells. For a global outer test, declare an incoming interplanetary approach class explicitly, apply the sight-line constraints and give an upper diameter for that class, including all numerical failures in the accounting. Evaluate timing offsets up to one second and station offsets up to one arcsecond separately from the nominal cap. Evaluate additional bounded accelerations explicitly. A forced change in assumptions must stay visible.

For a second historical arc, use all archived TESS 703/G96 optical rows on September 9, 2018 as training and score the first G96 exposure on September 10. Use the nominal 2 arcsecond cap and repeat at 3 arcseconds. Use 4.4 degrees as a standardized test circle only. Fit with training angles only. Use this as a retrospective held-out direction on a distinct arc of a previously studied mission. Compare both missions against local propagated covariance and a range/range-rate grid with the same optical model and an equal maximum count of model evaluations per method. Time and actual evaluation counts must also be reported. Fixed grid results are sampling evidence. Check the square geometry separately. Do not use the reconstructed spacecraft state or score directions to select an upper-bound prior.
